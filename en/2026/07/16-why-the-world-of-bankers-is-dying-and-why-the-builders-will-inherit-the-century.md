Why the World of Bankers Is Dying, and Why the Builders Will Inherit the Century
by Brivael Le Pogam, posted on July 7th, 2026

We are at a historic turning point.
And I am deeply convinced that this turning point will give birth to a radically better world. Not "better" in the sense of the Davos slogans. Better in the most concrete sense there is: more just, more efficient, more true.
To understand where we are going, we first have to look coldly at where we came from.
The old world: takers and bankers
For decades, the world was run by two castes. The takers and the bankers. Those who take, and those who finance the taking.
Their alliance never produced value. It produced systems. Layered systems, narratives stacked one on top of the other, designed not to serve but to extract. An entire architecture whose primary function was never to create, but to capture — capture the rent, capture the attention, capture the power, and then make itself indispensable to the very flow it had diverted.
That world does not manufacture builders. It manufactures figures.
Men shaped by the machine — selected, packaged, propelled by the interwoven networks of finance, media, and institutions. Macron, Obama: pure products of that software. Brilliant managers of nothing. They never built anything that exists in the real world. They administered, they embodied, they performed a role written elsewhere, by others. The talent was real — but it was the talent of the actor, not the engineer.
And the people sensed it. Confusedly but surely. They sensed that rightness had left the systems. That something rang false in the machinery. That is where the permanent tension of our era comes from: from that shared intuition that the ones making the decisions are neither competent, nor legitimate, nor in contact with the real.
Ray Dalio spent his life studying the cycles of great empires. His conclusion is chilling, and fits in one sentence: civilizations almost never die from their external enemies. They rot from within — through the decadence of their elites, the financialization of everything, and the silent collapse of the quality of their decisions. That is precisely where we are. Incompetence is no longer the accident in the system. It has become the operating system.
The other species of man: the builder

Naval, the philosopher of Silicon Valley, put it in a single line that went around the world: "It's the Age of Builders. (sorry financiers and talkers.)"
Pause for a second on that near-perfect mirror. His financiers and talkers are exactly my bankers and my takers. Two men who never coordinated, one rigorously identical intuition. That is the sign that what we are describing here is not an opinion or an ideological whim — but a tectonic shift that everyone, by now, can feel rising beneath their feet.
Facing that caste, there has always existed another species of man. The builder.
The one who doesn't narrate the world, but constructs it. The one for whom truth is not an opinion to impose but a constraint to respect. The real does not negotiate: the rocket flies or it explodes. The car drives or it doesn't. The software runs or it crashes. No narrative, no network, no glass of champagne will save an object that doesn't work. That is what makes the builder incorruptible where the taker is infinitely malleable: he answers to something greater than himself.
And the absolute incarnation of the builder of the last twenty years — the one who condenses the entire paradigm shift into a single figure — is Elon Musk.
Elon Musk, the man who changed the rules
You have to measure what he truly represents.
Musk is not simply an entrepreneur who succeeded. He is the living proof that the old world was a bluff. That you could build things deemed impossible — catching a rocket stage vertically, industrializing the electric car, connecting the planet from orbit — not despite refusing to play the game of corporate bullshit, but because you refuse to play it.
He never entered the status game. Never entered the hushed theater of boardrooms, networks of the obliged, favors returned. Where the old world optimizes for appearance, he optimizes for the real. Where the old world hires on pedigree, he hires on the ability to deliver. That obsession with the concrete — almost monomaniacal — is exactly what the takers were always incapable of understanding, and exactly what renders them obsolete.
But Musk's deepest move lies elsewhere. It is neither SpaceX nor Tesla.
It is having understood that the last territory left to conquer was not industrial, but narrative.
For decades, builders reigned over production and stayed silent on the story. They manufactured the real, but let the takers write its history. By buying the public square, by refusing to leave the monopoly of discourse to the caste that had always held it, Musk did something no builder had ever dared to do: he carried the war onto the terrain of ideas. He tore the takers' last citadel out of their hands.
That is why he is hated with such intensity. Not because he is wrong. Because he proves, every day, in public, that the emperor has no clothes.
An anecdote that says everything
Something I lived from the inside, that captures the whole shift better than any essay.
When I was at Y Combinator, the overwhelming majority of events had nothing pompous about them. No canapés, no champagne flutes, no socialite ritual. Burritos, pizzas, a bit of beer. We talked product, we talked billing, we talked execution. Pragmatic to the bone — because that is the real culture of builders: the real first, decorum never.
The only pompous events were the ones where we received investors — those people who came from the hushed old world. Then, yes, out came the canapés, the champagne, and the sharp suits. Then we played the status game. We put on the costume.
But that's exactly the point: it's a costume. A garment you put on to speak the language of the old world for the length of a meeting — not an identity, not a culture, not a way of being. The taker is his costume. The builder puts it on and takes it off. The entire civilizational difference lives in that detail.
That world is dying
Let's say it plainly: the old world is in agony.
That is precisely why it resists with such violence. A dying system does not go quietly — it bites, it locks down, it demonizes, it criminalizes. What we mistake for power is often just the clenching of a body that refuses to die.
But deep down, it already knows it has lost.
Because the people have changed their demand. They no longer ask for speeches or symbols: they ask for services. More and more efficient, more and more concrete, faster and faster. And the elites know, better than anyone, that they are structurally incapable of delivering them. You do not manufacture efficiency with people trained to manage narrative. That terrain belongs to the builders — and like everything they eventually touch, they will take it by proof, not by permission.
AI reshuffled the deck
And yet one last line of defense remained.
The realm of ideas, of concepts, of theory. The one terrain where the takers kept a real advantage, because producing narratives at scale was a craft unto itself, reserved for their caste — journalists, communicators, television intellectuals, opinion-makers. The builder, historically, was mute. He knew how to do, but not how to say. He could build an industrial empire without ever weighing in on the cultural battle.
AI just blew that lock off.
Now the one who builds can also think, write, structure, and distribute — at scale, without intermediaries, without begging for access to the microphone. The monopoly on storytelling has just collapsed. The builder is no longer condemned to silence. He steps into the war of ideas in turn, and he is going to win it, for one simple and unanswerable reason: he speaks from the real. He isn't defending an abstraction; he is describing what he built with his own hands.
It is the takers' final rampart falling. And it is the one they clung to most.
The real reset
That is why the true reset is not the one we were sold.
The bankers' "Great Reset" was a preservation operation disguised as transformation: keeping power by changing the vocabulary, locking down a dying order under the language of progress.
Ours is the exact opposite. It is a liberation. The transfer of power from those who narrate to those who do. From manufactured figures to real men. From systems of extraction to machines of creation.
The Great Great Reset.
And it has already begun.
