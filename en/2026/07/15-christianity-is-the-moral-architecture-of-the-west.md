Christianity Is the Moral Architecture of the West
by Brivael Le Pogam, posted on July 5th, 2026

I am baptized, I have received communion.

I have never been very observant.

I am not going to pretend that I have spent my life in church or that I am a religious model.

But one thing is clear: on matters of values, Christianity is probably the most perfect foundation the West has ever produced.

Not only as a religion.

As a moral architecture.

As a system of values capable of sustaining a free, prosperous, and humane civilization.

And the more I observe the world, the more I think that many people still do not understand how deeply the core values of Christianity are aligned with the core values of capitalism.

Not the caricatured capitalism of “everything for money”.

True capitalism: individual responsibility, freedom, personal dignity, value creation, merit, work, risk-taking, transmission.

Christianity begins with an absolutely revolutionary idea: every individual has infinite value.

Not “the group”.
Not “the class”.
Not “the mass”.
Not “the abstract collective”.
The individual.
Every soul counts.
Every person is responsible for their actions.
Every human being can fall, rise again, create, repair, choose the good, carry their cross, transform their condition.
This is exactly the opposite of communist anthropology.

The communism views the human being as an interchangeable fragment of a group: a social class, a category, a mass to be administered.

Christianity sees the human being as a unique person, endowed with a conscience, a responsibility and a vocation.
And that is where the link with capitalism becomes evident.
Capitalism rests on the idea that the individual can create something that transcends their initial condition.

A craftsman.
An entrepreneur.
An inventor.
An engineer.
A merchant.
A builder.

Someone who takes a risk, works, serves others, creates value, and receives in return the consequence of what they have built.

It is profoundly human.

Human nature is not made to be flattened into an artificial equality.

Individuals are not born with the same talents, the same energy, the same vision, the same discipline, the same courage, the same capacity to bear chaos.

Some people are exceptional.

And a healthy civilization must not lower them.
It must channel them, make them responsible, elevate them, and allow them to build.
This is how we get cathedrals, businesses, technologies, works, discoveries, cities, industries, productive empires.

Christianity does not say: “be mediocre so as not to disturb the group”.
It says: “you have received talents, do something with them”.
The parable of the talents is probably one of the most anti-communist texts ever written.

The one who buries his talent out of fear, passivity or cowardice is condemned.

The one who makes it bear fruit is rewarded.

This is exactly the entrepreneurial logic.

Receive.
Work.
Multiply.
Transmit.

Communism, for its part, hates this idea because it hates exception.

It prefers to punish the one who produces rather than hold accountable the one who does not produce.
It prefers to organize envy rather than celebrate excellence.

It prefers to talk about equality while creating a bureaucratic caste that decides for everyone.

And that is where Christianity and capitalism come together on a fundamental point: freedom does not exist without responsibility.

You are free.
But you are responsible.

You can succeed.
But you must serve.

You can own.
But you must transmit.

You can become powerful.
But you will be judged on the use of your power.

Capitalism without morality becomes cynicism.

But Christianity without freedom becomes disguised socialism.
The beauty of the West came precisely from this fertile tension:

A free economy.
An individual responsibility.
A Christian morality.
A human dignity.
An admiration for those who build.
A mistrust of collective tyrannies.

This foundation produced Western civilization.

Not resentment.
Not forced egalitarianism.
Not hatred of the rich.
Not the negation of merit.
Not bureaucracy.
Not the cult of the victim.

Christianity elevated the individual.
Capitalism gave him a field to create.
And the West exploded in science, art, industry, technology, prosperity.

Communism, for its part, tried to replace God with the State, responsibility with excuse, the individual with the group, merit with forced redistribution, and charity with administration.

Result: wherever it passes, it destroys the soul, the economy, the family, freedom and truth.

I am not saying that every Christian is a capitalist, nor that every capitalist is a Christian.

I am saying something deeper:

A free civilization needs a moral foundation.

And the West had found that foundation in Christianity.
When we remove God, responsibility, family, merit, transcendence and the dignity of the individual, what remains is not a freer society.

What remains is a void.
And that void is always filled by the State, ideology, bureaucracy, resentment and control.

The real subject is there.
A civilization that denies the individual always ends up denying the human.

A civilization that celebrates the exceptional individual, while reminding him of his moral responsibility, can build miracles.

That is why Christianity and capitalism are not enemies.

They are two pillars of the same world:

The dignity of the individual.
And his capacity to create.
