Meta Is Already One Of AI's Biggest Winners
by Austin Koring, posted on July 17th, 2026

Meta is one of the largest beneficiaries of artificial intelligence (AI) in the world right now. It is just not obvious because the payoff is not showing up in a new product category. It shows up inside an advertising auction that most people never think about.
The Ad Engine Is Already An AI System
Meta’s core business is selling attention. A person opens Instagram, and in the milliseconds before the feed loads, a ranking system processes thousands of signals to predict which content and ads that person is most likely to engage with. The advertiser who wins that auction pays based on predicted conversion probability. The quality of that prediction is the product. This has been true for years. What has changed is how good those predictions have become and how much attention they can hold.
The history of Meta’s ad engine is a history of compounding AI improvements that never made the front page. The first generation of ranking systems used simple feature-based models. Then came deep learning recommendation models that could process dense and sparse features simultaneously. Then came sequence-based architectures that look at behavioral patterns over time, like what you share, who you engage with, and the posts you interact with, rather than static snapshots of who a user is. Each transition improved prediction quality. Each improvement in prediction quality made Meta more valuable to advertisers. Better targeting lifted return on ad spend (ROAS), and higher ROAS pulled more dollars onto the platform. For years, revenue followed quietly behind the model improvements.
The recent product names are different pieces of the same compounding curve. Advantage+ sits closest to the advertiser. It moves more of the workflow into Meta’s systems, from campaign setup and audience selection to budget allocation and creative testing. By Q3 2023, Meta said Advantage+ Shopping campaigns had reached a $10 billion run rate, and more than half of advertisers were using Advantage+ Creative tools. (Source: Meta Q3 2023 earnings call)
Andromeda is the first cut inside the auction. From tens of millions of possible ads, it helps Meta find the few thousand candidates worth considering. Generative Ads Model (GEM) comes next, powering the ranking stage that decides which of those candidates is most likely to matter to the user. Lattice moves more of the learning from narrow models built for specific ad tasks into a larger shared ranking system, so a lesson from one part of Meta’s ad engine can improve the rest.
In Q2 2025, Meta said Andromeda enhancements drove nearly 4% higher conversions on Facebook mobile Feed and Reels, GEM improvements increased ad conversions by approximately 5% on Instagram and 3% on Facebook Feed and Reels, and recent Lattice deployments drove a nearly 4% increase in ad conversions across Facebook Feed and Reels. (Source: Meta Q2 2025 earnings call)
Adaptive Ranking is another step in the same direction. Meta describes it as a Large Language Model (LLM)-scale ads recommender model used at inference, meaning it helps make the decision when an ad is actually being served. Instead of sending every ad request through the same model, Adaptive Ranking routes each request to the model best suited to the user, context, and probability of conversion, while still meeting the sub-second speed required for Meta’s feeds. Meta said Adaptive Ranking launched on Instagram in Q4 2025 and delivered a 3% increase in ad conversions and a 5% increase in ad click-through rate for targeted users. In Q1 2026, Meta expanded Adaptive Ranking to support offsite conversions, driving a 1.6% increase in conversion rates across major Facebook and Instagram surfaces. (Sources: Meta Engineering, March 2026; Meta Q1 2026 earnings call)
Those numbers do not show up as a new revenue line. They show up as better ad performance across 3.56 billion daily active people. This is what AI monetization looks like at scale: a marginal improvement in ad quality, multiplied across billions of daily interactions. (Source: Meta Q1 2026 earnings call)
The chart below summarizes the two variables that matter most for the ad engine: how many ads Meta serves and how much it earns for the average ad. In terms of supply-and-demand, impressions are supply, while average price per ad is a proxy for advertiser demand, or how much advertisers are willing to pay for Meta’s inventory. If Meta creates more impressions faster than advertisers can absorb them, price per ad tends to fall. But when impressions and price rise together, it means advertisers are absorbing more supply at higher prices. Since late 2023, Meta has consistently grown both at once. That does not prove AI explains every dollar of growth, but it is where better prediction, ranking, and advertiser returns would be expected to appear in the financials.
 

The Growth Problem AI Is Solving
To understand why this matters, start with Meta’s growth constraint. With International Telecommunication Union (ITU) estimating ~6 billion people online in 2025 and DataReportal estimating ~1.1 billion of those in China, Meta's 3.56 billion daily active people already represent ~73% penetration of the ~4.9 billion global internet population excluding China. Against that backdrop, Meta’s 3.56 billion daily active people in March 2026 represents roughly 73% penetration of the ex-China internet population. The denominator is imperfect, but the direction is clear: on its current family of apps, Meta is already deeply penetrated across the internet population it can realistically serve. User growth is no longer the main engine. The next leg of growth has to come from making each existing user more valuable.
 

The average revenue per person (ARPP) gap shown above is where that opportunity lives. U.S. and Canada users generate roughly $71 per person per quarter. Asia-Pacific users generate about $10. Rest of World users generate about $5. Instagram and Facebook are not fundamentally different products in those markets, but advertiser density, measurement quality, commerce infrastructure, and model confidence are very different. Closing even a fraction of that gap through better AI-driven targeting and creative optimization could be worth tens of billions of dollars in annual revenue without requiring Meta to add another billion users.
The ARPP gap is the opportunity, and the ROAS flywheel is how Meta works to close it. Advantage+, GEM, Lattice, Andromeda, and adaptive ranking all improve the same loop: better audience selection, better creative testing, better retrieval, better ranking, and better conversion prediction. When the loop works, click-through rates and conversion rates rise, advertiser returns improve, and more budget flows into Meta’s auction. More spend creates more measured outcomes, giving the next model better data. Compute accelerates the flywheel by making the prediction engine sharper. That is why we believe Meta’s AI payoff is already showing up inside the ad auction, where better prediction makes each impression worth more.
