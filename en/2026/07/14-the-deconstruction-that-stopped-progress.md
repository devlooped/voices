The Deconstruction That Stopped Progress
by Brivael Le Pogam, posted on July 2nd, 2026

I was asked yesterday how someone building an AI company ends up talking about Foucault, French Theory, and wokism on X.

The real answer is more interesting than the question. Because I never decided to talk about that. I arrived there out of necessity. By pulling on a thread. And the thread led me much further than I imagined.

I'm going to tell you the sequence, because it says something about the way a builder thinks — and about why it's often the builders, and not the professional intellectuals, who end up seeing clearly.

It all starts with a character trait: I've always been an entrepreneur at heart. Since I was a kid. And there's something the commentator will never understand and that the builder learns in his flesh: when you build something real, you don't have the right to lie to yourself. The market doesn't read your intentions, it reads your results. If your theory of the world is wrong, you don't lose a debate on X — you lose your company, your money, your nights. That constraint, that skin in the game, is the best bullshit detector ever invented. It forces you to understand the world as it is, not as it should be.

So naturally, while building, I started digging into economics. How value is created. How prices circulate. How a system optimizes. And there, first shock.

Because we know. Since Friedman, since Hayek, we know how an efficient system works: you let prices tell the truth, you let millions of people freely exchange their information in the form of transactions, and you remove the State from the mechanics. It's not an opinion, it's almost a physical law — the knowledge problem, spontaneous order. We've demonstrated it a hundred times. And yet, everywhere, even in the United States, even in the most capitalist country on Earth, we continue to live in administered, planned, mortally regulated economies, bottle-fed on subsidies. We know the remedy, we hold it in our hand, and we refuse to swallow it.

That's the first mystery. And it drove me crazy. Because an engineer, faced with a system that malfunctions when the solution is known, asks only one question: what's blocking it?

Second thread. If the remedy has been known for half a century, why are there still, in 2026, Marxists? Not just a few isolated nostalgics — entire university departments, newsrooms, ministries. How can an idea refuted by History itself, by a hundred million dead, by the methodical collapse of every regime that applied it, survive intact precisely in the most prestigious institutions of the West? A false idea should die. This one prospers. Why?

Third thread, and it's Thiel who gives me the key. The world has been blocked since the 70s. We were promised flying cars, we got 280 characters. We walked on the Moon in 69 and never went back. Atomic growth stopped dead — energy, transport, space, medicine — and only bit growth continued. A civilization that, for the first time in its history, has stopped believing in its own future. The decline is not economic first. It is spiritual. A failure of faith.

At this stage, I have three symptoms on the table. An economy we refuse to optimize even though we know how. A dead ideology that refuses to die. A civilization that has stopped advancing. And the builder's instinct, the one who has been debugging code since adolescence, tells me only one thing: these are not three problems. It's one. There is a common upstream cause. Find the root.

So I go back up. And I stumble upon it.

French Theory. Foucault, Derrida, Deleuze. Three brilliant Frenchmen who, in the ruins of post-68, manufactured in the elegance of our language the most effective deconstruction machine in History. The message fits in three axe blows. Foucault: there is no truth, only power relations disguised as knowledge. Derrida: there is no stable meaning, every text undoes itself, every heritage dissolves. Deleuze: prefer the nomad to the sedentary, desire to the law, becoming to being.

And suddenly, everything fits back together. Everything.

Because a civilization stands on three pillars: the belief that there exists a truth accessible to reason, the belief that there exists a good distinct from evil, the belief that there exists a heritage worth transmitting. Truth, merit, transmission. And French Theory set out, methodically, to dynamite the three.

That's why we refuse to optimize the economy: because we've learned to see merit as 'domination' and success as 'violence'. That's why Marxism doesn't die: because we've unlearned to judge an idea by its results, only 'narratives' in competition remain. That's why the world is blocked: because an entire generation has learned to suspect and never to admire, to deconstruct and never to build, to see power everywhere and beauty nowhere.

Wokism is only the most visible, most grotesque form of the symptom. These Parisian texts, unreadable to us, crossed the Atlantic in the 80s, married American puritanism and racial guilt, and spawned this monster. The matrix is French. We exported doubt the way others export weapons.

And the final twist, the most cynical: this deconstruction software, forged by a left that hated power, is today being recovered by the globalists themselves. Because a people without common truth, without pride, without heritage to defend, without anything solid under their feet, is a people infinitely easier to administer. A population of rootless nomads who no longer know in the name of what to say no. Deconstruction was supposed to liberate; it has manufactured the perfect herd.

That's how you go from an AI company to Michel Foucault. It's not a detour, it's not an intellectual's hobby. It's exactly the same subject, the same question asked on two different floors: what prevents building — and how to fix it?

And the answer will not be written in a colloquium. It is being written right now, in AI labs, in startups, in workshops, everywhere people are still making real things instead of commenting on them. A civilization is not rebuilt by those who suspect. It is rebuilt by those who build, who assume a hierarchy of the beautiful, the true, the good, and who are not ashamed to transmit it.
