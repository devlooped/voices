Mini-Manifesto: Why Socialism Cannot Serve the Common Good
by Brivael Le Pogam, posted on August 2nd, 2026

Mini-manifesto for those who still believe that socialism can serve the common good.

You have good intentions.
You want less injustice, less misery, more dignity.
You think the market is too cruel, too unequal, too "selfish," and that the state must take back control to force the common good.

This manifesto does not attack your compassion.
It attacks the tool you propose.

Axioms (non-negotiable)

1. Human beings respond to incentives.
   If you reward effort, innovation, and risk, you get more of them.
   If you punish them or force equality of outcomes, you get less. This is universal. It is not a right-wing opinion.

2. Knowledge is dispersed.
   No one (neither party, nor committee, nor state algorithm) can know the millions of needs, tastes, costs, and opportunities that exist at every moment. Hayek demonstrated it: the central planner is condemned to blindness.

3. Without free market prices, economic calculation is impossible.
   Mises said it as early as 1920. Without private property and without profit and loss, you do not know what is scarce, what is demanded, what is wasted. You navigate by sight... and you crash.

4. The "common good" is not a menu the state can impose.
   It emerges when millions of free people pursue their own goals under stable rules (property, contracts, non-aggression). Every attempt to manufacture it by decree ends up destroying it.

The data (not the slogans)

- North Korea / South Korea
  Same people, same culture, same starting point in 1953.
  One chose real socialism. The other the market.
  Today: about 1,200 to 1,500 dollars of GDP per capita in the North versus about 36,000 dollars in the South. A difference of roughly 25 to 30 times. One has recurring famines and camps. The other is a technological power.

- Venezuela
  The richest country in Latin America for decades thanks to oil.
  After 20 years of "21st-century socialism" (Chávez then Maduro): GDP collapse of 70 to 80 percent, hyperinflation, over 90 percent poverty at the peak (ENCOVI), 7 to 8 million refugees.
  It was not "badly applied." It was applied.

- The great socialist famines
  Holodomor (Ukraine 1932-33): 3.5 to 5 million dead.
  Great Leap Forward (China 1958-62): 30 to 45 million dead.
  Both man-made. Both under collectivization and central planning.

- The exit from global misery
  Extreme global poverty has collapsed since 1980.
  The vast majority of that drop happened where markets were allowed to work (China after 1978, India after 1991, Southeast Asia, and so on).
  Countries that maintained total control of the economy stayed poor or regressed.

The implacable logic

Socialism promises equality of outcomes.
To get it, it must destroy the incentives that produce wealth.
When production collapses, all that remains is to ration scarcity... and repress those who complain.
That is why, historically, real socialism has always ended up producing: shortage plus waiting lines plus political police plus Party privileges.

You can call it "not real socialism."
But every time it was tried seriously (USSR, Maoist China, Cambodia, Cuba, Ethiopia, Venezuela, Nicaragua...), the same pattern appeared.
It is no longer a coincidence. It is a law.

What liberalism (the real kind) actually brings to the common good

- It does not promise equality of outcomes. It promises equality before the law and the freedom to try your luck.
- It produced the greatest exit from poverty in the history of humanity.
- It enables real (voluntary) charity and life-saving innovation (medicines, fertilizers, smartphones, vaccines...).
- It lets people judge what is good for them, instead of leaving a committee to decide in their place.

The market is not morally perfect.
It is simply the only system that aligns the interest of the individual with the creation of wealth for others, through mutual consent.

Conclusion:

If you truly want the common good, stop looking at intentions and look at results.
Socialism has a track record. It is catastrophic.
Liberalism has a track record. It is the greatest creator of prosperity and freedom ever seen.

You can keep believing that "this time it will be different."
Or you can accept that the axioms and the data have said the same thing for a century:

Socialism cannot work.
Not because socialists are evil.
But because reality does not obey them.

There you go.
Now you know.
